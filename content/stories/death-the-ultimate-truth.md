Since I was a kid, one question has followed me everywhere: *What is going on? What am I even experiencing?*

Nobody answered it. They just put me in a tunnel.

At the start, life is two things. Eat. Sleep. Then you learn to walk, you start talking to Amma and Nanna, and the world gets a little bigger.

Then one morning, someone puts a bag on your back and sends you to school. You make friends. That’s the good part. Eat, sleep, play. Then one more thing gets added: study. And things get hard.

The race begins. Good marks. Better marks. Somewhere in there, you fall in love for the first time. It fails. First love always does.

Plus one, plus two. Entrance exams. Engineering. Four more levels: first year, second, third, fourth. One goal now: get the job.

You get the job.

And it feels exactly like first grade again. Junior. Senior. Lead. Manager. CTO. Ten more levels. A new tunnel inside the old one.

Now the list gets longer. Fancy CTC. A bike. An iPhone in your pocket. A Rare Rabbit shirt. Cool photos on Instagram. Weekends at a mid-tier pub, drinking enough to forget the week. Then Monday comes, and it starts again.

Your 30s arrive. A few grey hairs. You’re told it’s time to get married, so you do. Kids come next.

And one day you notice: you’ve become your father. Your child has become you. Same tunnel, different timeline.

Then you’re 70. And one day, without warning, everything goes dark. Like falling asleep. Funny part? You don’t even know you’re dead.

> Our friends die. Our children die. Our grandchildren die.

This is the cycle that has been happening for millions of years. And it is unavoidable.

Now, if we stop for a second and think about it, why is all of this happening? Why was I born?

![If you’ve seen the ocean at 6:30 a.m., you’re blessed.](media:de8ede33-8d4b-476d-a1bf-6a38fe81908f)

I’m an accidental arrangement of matter, right? I was born through an unintentional combination of circumstances. My existence wasn’t planned by me. I’m just a random human being who came into existence.

Then people gave me a name. They told me to go to school. I made friends, grew up, and started living my life. And that’s it. It’s a cycle.

> Then, one fine day, I will die. Maybe on a happy day, surrounded by the people who love me. And that’s it. The chapter closes.

So why was I born? Just to die? To give birth to someone who is also going to die? To love someone when I know that one day I might lose them? To make friends and create beautiful memories, only to eventually miss each other and face the same fate? To be buried in a grave, reduced to ashes, or returned to the earth?

![Humans, flowers, greenery, the sky, and mountains. We need to protect this frame as it is, so that someone in the year 3079 can experience the same feeling you’re experiencing now, right?](media:0f9c0077-2295-4d28-a511-d8cac7b0a43c)

The funny part is that you don’t know when you will die.

We spend our lives worrying about money, even though we know we won’t take any of it with us when we die.

We worry every day about so many things.

What will people think if I walk down the row while everyone is watching? What will someone think of me if I post this on Instagram? What will people think if I marry this girl or boy? What will people think if I don't have the right music taste, or if I listen to a particular song in public?

What will people think if I get married in a less fancy convention hall and serve average food?

![I think the sky is in a bad mood. Looks like a scene before a cyclone… or is it just blushing?](media:75846e86-54d1-494c-b6f7-34b1d691b999)

There are so many situations in which we consciously or unconsciously worry about other people’s opinions, forgetting one simple truth: I’m going to die one day.

Just hold back for a moment. Close your eyes and say it slowly.

> “I’m going to die one day.”

If you’re panicking, feeling tense, anxious, or depressed about something, whatever it may be, remember this: death is the ultimate truth.

And the fact that we are alive gives us the opportunity to try to be happier, to experience life, and to do the things we want to do.

So, in the grand scheme of things, does any of this matter?

If you think you want popularity, fame, or recognition, ask yourself: Do you even remember the names of famous people who lived centuries ago?

![These mountains have witnessed every phase of humanity. They have seen different eras, the rise and fall of civilizations, and countless wars. They have watched people come and go, each searching for something of their own.](media:6207af24-c10a-4280-b98e-97270e1ab827)

And through it all, the mountains have stood still, watching.

What is this?

We don’t care about most of them anymore.

In the same way, people might not remember you after 100 years, 2,000 years, or a million years. We don’t even know what humanity will look like that far into the future. The work we do today might eventually disappear.

The deadline is fixed. The only certainty is that we are going to die.

Then why should I live? What should my motivation be?

Bro, here’s the magic: You’re going to die one day anyway.

The day will eventually end. The years will pass. And one day, you will lose someone you love. That’s part of life.

> So, what is the point of all this?

Should I build something like ChatGPT, knowing that it might become irrelevant 500 years from now? If everything I create will eventually disappear, what's the point?

We are born, perhaps by accident. We are going to die, and we don’t know what happens after death.

One thing is certain: it doesn’t make sense to stop working simply because we’re going to die someday. But does that mean we should spend our entire lives chasing money, fame, and success?

Not necessarily.

Having money makes certain things easier. Without it, life becomes difficult. You need food, shelter, and other necessities. Banks will come asking for their payments. Your house owner will ask for rent. Your manager will call you about your work. You might lose your job. Your customers will ask why your business hasn’t opened for days. Your mother might ask why you’re not attending family events. Your father will worry about you and wonder why you're not as active as before.

Whether we like it or not, we are already part of this cycle. We have responsibilities, needs, and expectations to deal with.

![It all begins with the sunrise. The Sun grows hotter, summer arrives, water evaporates, clouds gather, and rain falls. Eventually, winter comes. Our lives, our routines, and the cycles of nature are all connected to the Sun.](media:e17a75ad-ce74-4581-aba9-9f720b17c451)

So, we have two choices.

Either we try to live a good life — a happier, healthier life, with enough money to support ourselves and the freedom to experience the world — or we sit in a room, doing nothing, thinking about the fact that we are going to die one day.

Either way, we are going to die.

So why should we feel dead every second from now on? Why should we bury ourselves alive before our time comes?

> The goal is not to avoid death. The goal is to live well before we die.

> Accept that death is inevitable.

So then, what is the ultimate truth?

Should I stop worrying about my manager tasks? Should I stop worrying about my career choices? Should I stop being ambitious? Should I not get married? Should I stop traveling? Should I not care about my parents? No. I feel like there is no answer. Just feel it, like an experience. Experience everything that is there right now, in this day, between sunrise and sunset.

The only truth is that there is one sunrise and one sunset. Beyond that, there is nothing. The past has already happened; it’s like a written page. The future is like an empty page. There is nothing in it yet. Past and future are just words invented for literature. What’s real is that you are alive now. What you are saying is the truth. What you are seeing, the people you are talking to, the air you breathe: that is the truth.

![You can’t escape the light, even when you’re under the trees.](media:c59628bc-af53-4a8a-ac2a-9068d2b9c379)

So what should I do now? I feel like I should just do things and experience them first. Instead of sitting in a room feeling depressed, go outside, travel more and stay positive. Keep in mind that you are going to die, but always chase the good feeling.

How do I know when I have that good feeling? It’s waking up early in the morning and watching the sun rise. It’s feeling the cold morning air slowly turn warm. It’s watching the rain from your window. It’s sitting on a beach at 5:17 p.m., talking with your friends and watching the sunset. Talking to someone you love. Always chase that good feeling.

So what should I chase? A paycheck that gives me access to things? That one travel destination? Whatever makes you feel good. Maybe it’s earning money. Maybe it’s talking to people, like your high school friend. Maybe it’s walking or running. Maybe it’s watching films. Maybe it’s talking to your Amma. Maybe it’s achieving something or learning a new skill. Maybe it’s selling fruit from a cart. Maybe it’s buying groceries. Maybe it’s traveling to new places; that makes me feel good. Maybe it’s just sitting and thinking. Maybe it’s playing a sport like cricket or badminton, hitting a six and getting that main-character aura.

These are the things that make you feel good. So make a list, or just observe what makes you feel good. Noticing what makes you feel good is the important part. That’s the first step.

Now you know what makes you feel good. So what’s next? You live in a world where everything is expensive, and most things on your list need money. So you need to earn money to do the things that make you feel good. You are the priority. You are earning so that you can do those things.

And it isn’t always money. If you want to walk, you need healthy legs. If you want to play, you need good health. If you want to travel, you need money. If you want a job, you need knowledge. If you want to start a business, you need capital, an idea and customers. So notice what makes you feel good and understand what it takes to keep it. Then repeat it. The goal is to have hundreds of good moments every day, every week.

![Wake up, watch the sunrise, work, eat, chit-chat, watch the sunset, count the stars, and sleep.](media:b02e0f2c-eb04-47c1-9987-e30f3112ac34)

Even washing dishes needs water, a Vim bar and a little money. So first, observe what makes you feel good. Then work out what it takes to sustain it for a long time. That's the second step.

The third step is to do what you love. That’s it. Forget everything else and just do it. Be ruthless about it. If nothing else works, tell yourself, “I’m going to die one day, so I choose to live well.” Nothing else. Produce good work that people appreciate. Do what you love, and repeat it. Maybe that’s building a company or doing great work.

Most of your time goes to work, so you need to love what you do. In today’s world, you need money for everything: to pay your parents’ bills, to stay healthy, for all of it. A thousand years ago, the person who could hunt was happier because they could solve their problems. But now you have to learn something in order to earn something. You are either learning or earning, and you earn so that you can feel good.

You spend part of your life sleeping, and most of the rest working. Work, sleep, and the people you love: these three are what ultimately matter. So choose work you love most, where you feel in full flow, completely absorbed in it. Your profession matters too.
