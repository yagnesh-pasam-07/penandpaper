Today you'll probably post a WhatsApp status.

Maybe a story. *Happy Andhra Pradesh Formation Day.* A flag. A filter. A song in the background.

But pause for one second.

Have you ever wondered why there is a state called Andhra Pradesh at all? Why the map of India was drawn by language? Why the words you speak at home, *amma*, *annam*, *intiki vellu*, have a land of their own?

Someone paid for that.

Not with money. With his life.

If you're even a little curious, you should know this man.

His name is Potti Sriramulu.

---

Go back to 1901.

A boy is born in Madras on 16 March, into a Telugu family whose roots are in the Nellore country.

Nothing about him looks like history. He studies. He works hard. He goes to Bombay, trains as a sanitary engineer, and gets a job with the Great Indian Peninsula Railway.

A railway job in those days meant something. A fixed salary. Respect in the family. A future you could see clearly.

He marries. A child is on the way.

An ordinary man with ordinary dreams.

Hold on to that picture. Because it is about to break.

[IMAGE: The engineer — a young Sriramulu at a 1920s Bombay railway office, steam trains in the background]

---

It is 1928. He is 27.

His wife dies giving birth.

A few days later, the baby dies too.

In one week, the whole future he was building is gone. The house is quiet in a way it was never meant to be.

Most people, after a loss like that, slowly rebuild the same life. A new home. Perhaps a new marriage. The same job, the same road. No one would blame him.

He chooses something else.

Two years later, after his mother also passes, he resigns from the railway. He walks away from the salary, the security, the respectable life.

Why would a man walk away from everything he had?

Maybe because grief had already taught him what most of us learn too late: that life is short, and the only real question is what you spend it on.

[IMAGE: The loss — a grieving man alone by a window, rain outside, an empty cradle in the shadows]

---

He goes to Gandhiji.

At the Sabarmati Ashram, life is simple to the point of hardness. Spinning. Cleaning. Prayer. Sleeping on the floor. Eating little.

He marches in the Salt Satyagraha. He is arrested and jailed, more than once.

And in Nellore he does something that tells you exactly who he is. The Venugopala Swami temple keeps Dalits outside its doors, as it has for generations. He fasts until the doors open.

They open.

Gandhiji is said to have remarked that if he had eleven more followers like Sriramulu, he could win freedom in a year.

Remember that. A man who would starve for other people's dignity. That is not a small thing. It is the whole story, in miniature.

[IMAGE: The disciple — Sabarmati Ashram at dawn, a spinning wheel, Gandhian simplicity]

---

1947. India is free.

But for the Telugu people, something still hurts.

They are one people, with one language, but they don't have one home. The coastal and Rayalaseema districts sit inside the big Madras State, governed from Madras. The Telangana districts sit inside Hyderabad State.

In offices and courts, their *bhasha*, their language, is a guest in someone else's house.

Picture a farmer who walks into a government office with a land problem. The forms are in a language he can't read. He nods. He signs. He goes home not knowing what he agreed to.

For years, leaders promise a Telugu state. Committees meet. Reports are written.

Nothing happens.

How long can a people be asked to wait?

[IMAGE: The people — Telugu villagers, farmers and students holding handwritten Telugu banners]

**19 October 1952. Madras.**

In a house in Mylapore, at 126 Royapettah High Road, the home of the freedom fighter Bulusu Sambamurthy, a thin man in white khadi sits down.

He is 51 now. Grey hair. Spectacles.

He makes a vow. He will not eat until there is a separate state for the Telugu people. *Telugu vari kosam.* For the Telugu people.

There is no camera crew. No big speech.

Just a man, a mat, and a decision.

[IMAGE: The vow — a frail man in white khadi seated on a simple mat, a small oil lamp, morning light through a window]

---

**Day 1.** The hunger is sharp. He drinks water and sits quietly.

**Day 10.** The hunger fades. The body begins to live on itself.

**Day 20.** He has grown thin. Visitors come in a steady line. Some touch his feet. Some cry. Some beg him to stop.

**Day 30.** It hurts to sit up. He talks less now. Every word costs something.

In Delhi, the government watches and waits. They have seen fasts before. Fasts end. A promise is made, a glass of juice is offered, a photograph is taken.

Surely this one will end too.

**Day 40.** Across the Telugu districts, people follow the news the way you follow a relative in hospital. *What day is it now? How is he? Any word from Delhi?*

**Day 50.** His hand barely lifts. His voice is a whisper.

Outside the house, people stand in silence.

He does not give in.

[IMAGE: The slow burn — a frail man lying on a simple mat, a small oil lamp, a wall calendar with days crossed off]

---

**15 December 1952.**

The fifty-eighth day.

The room is quiet.

The man who once had a railway job, a wife, a child on the way, a whole ordinary life ahead of him, is lying on a mat in a borrowed room.

And then his breathing stops.

Potti Sriramulu is dead.

He was 51.

Fifty-eight days. Count them on a calendar sometime. It is the gap between one festival and the next. It is two monthly salaries.

It is the time it took for one man to give away the last thing he had.

That is what sacrifice means. Not a word in a speech. A body, slowly giving up, for people he would never meet.

[IMAGE: Night watch — outside the house in Madras, silent people holding candles in the dark]

---

The news spreads like fire through dry grass.

Grief becomes anger. Shops close. Towns shut down. Trains stop. In Anakapalle and Vijayawada, police open fire on protesters. Seven people are killed.

And then, four days after his death, on 19 December, Prime Minister Nehru stands up and announces it.

There will be a separate Andhra State.

On **1 October 1953**, Andhra State is born, with Kurnool as its capital. The first state in independent India made around a language.

His sacrifice forces a question onto the whole country: if Telugu people deserve a home for their language, why not everyone?

In 1956, India redraws its map by language. On **1 November 1956**, Andhra State and the Telugu districts of Hyderabad State come together.

Andhra Pradesh is born.

That is the day you are celebrating today.

[IMAGE: The map — South India glowing as Andhra's borders take shape, golden light rising like a sunrise]

---

And here is the saddest part.

He never saw any of it.

He never saw the state formed. He never saw a child in his own land learn in his own language. He never knew that people would one day call him *Amarajeevi*, the one who lives forever. He never knew that, in 2008, his home district would carry his name: Sri Potti Sriramulu Nellore.

He gave everything, and he never got to know what his sacrifice became.

Think about that for a moment.

Why would someone starve himself for people he would never meet? For children not yet born? For you?

There is no easy answer. Maybe he simply believed a language deserves a home, and that a people should not have to beg for it.

If he could see it today, the schools, the signboards, the films, your WhatsApp messages full of Telugu, would he smile?

Maybe the least we can do is know his name.

And know why.

[IMAGE: The legacy — his spirit made of soft light watching modern Andhra: temples, the Krishna river, school children reading Telugu, a gentle smile]

---

So, about that status.

Post it. Celebrate. It's a birthday, after all.

But before you do, remember who made the cake. A thin man in white khadi, on a mat in Madras, counting days nobody should have to count.

Before you post that status today, post his story instead.

Because a sacrifice is only wasted when it's forgotten.
